02 / About

Every issue starts as a blank page and a deadline. I like both.

I'm the designer at Tulip Magazine, where I take each issue from first sketch to the printer. That means covers, feature spreads, typography, art direction, and the hundred small decisions in between that you only notice when they're wrong.

I care about rhythm: how a reader moves from a loud spread to a quiet one, where the eye rests, and when a page should make you stop. Good editorial design shouldn't draw attention to itself. It should make the story feel inevitable.

When I'm not pushing pixels, I'm probably listening to In Rainbows on repeat.

03 / Process

How an issue comes together

Side A · 1

Read

Every story first. The layout should come out of the words, not get laid on top of them.

Side A · 2

Flatplan

Map the whole issue's pacing (loud, quiet, loud) before a single spread is designed.

Side B · 3

Design

Grids, type, image, and colour, iterated with editors until each page earns its place.

Side B · 4

Print

Proofs, colour checks, and one last obsessive pass before it goes out into the world.
